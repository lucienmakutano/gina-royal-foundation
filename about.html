Our Story

GINA Royal Foundation was established to address the profound challenges faced by widows in Machakos County, Kenya. In the small village of Malindi, we work directly with 30 remarkable women who have experienced loss and are courageously navigating the difficult journey of rebuilding their lives.

These women face not only emotional and psychological challenges but also practical difficulties in providing for their families. Many struggle with food insecurity due to irregular weather patterns, lack access to clean water, and have limited opportunities for sustainable income generation.

What began as a vision to help a few women has grown into a comprehensive, multigenerational initiative that transforms entire families and strengthens the fabric of the community.

Our Mantra

"Overcome grief and claim your dignity"

Grief and heartache can profoundly affect an individual, often leading to a sense of despair. A wounded spirit can diminish one's vitality, and where hope is absent, the heart suffers. This foundation aims to restore hope to widows, empowering them to overcome grief and reclaim their dignity.

We believe that every woman, regardless of her circumstances, deserves the opportunity to heal, grow, and thrive. Our mantra guides everything we do—from the programs we create to the partnerships we forge.

A Multigenerational Approach

GRF is a multigenerational foundation that serves women from the age of 30 to 90+ years old. This means that we work with 4 generations, each with unique needs, along with their children and grandchildren.

By taking a holistic, family-centered approach, we create lasting change that extends beyond individual widows to their entire families and communities.

When we uplift a woman, we uplift an entire community.

Why It Matters

In many parts of Kenya, widows face unique challenges including:

  • Social stigma and isolation
  • Loss of property and inheritance rights
  • Primary responsibility for children's welfare
  • Limited access to resources and opportunities
  • Emotional trauma without support systems

Our programs address these challenges head-on, providing both immediate relief and long-term solutions.

The Kintsugi Philosophy

Our approach is inspired by the Japanese art of Kintsugi—the practice of repairing broken pottery with golden lacquer, making the piece even more beautiful than before. The breaks and repairs become part of the object's history, rather than something to hide.

Similarly, we believe that the women we serve don't need to hide their pain or their past. Instead, we help them transform their experiences into sources of strength and beauty. Their resilience becomes their golden thread—making them not just restored, but even more remarkable than before.
