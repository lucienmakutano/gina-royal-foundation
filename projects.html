Our Projects & Initiatives

Five comprehensive programs creating sustainable change for widows, their families, and communities in Machakos County, Kenya.

GRF is a multigenerational Foundation serving women aged 30-90+ years, spanning 4 generations with their children and grandchildren. Each program is designed to address specific challenges while building toward long-term sustainability and community resilience.

Sustainable Farming Program

Q2 April - June: Kitchen Gardens Initiative

Due to irregular weather patterns, many widows struggle to harvest crops adequately and often rely on government relief food.

GRF is dedicated to empowering women by establishing small-scale kitchen gardens. This initiative was pitched this April and the women took it with open arms since it directly addresses household food security and enhances dietary diversity within communities.

September 2025: Expansion Phase

Building on this success, starting in September 2025, GRF will introduce sustainable farming techniques through sunflower cultivation and chicken rearing. This expansion aims to create long-term economic opportunities and resilience for the women.

Partnership Opportunity: GRF seeks strategic partnerships and collaboration with an oil-producing company that can purchase sunflower seeds from these women and help establish a sustainable business. This initiative not only supports the widows but also creates job opportunities for their children, making this foundation a transgenerational venture.

1. Education

GRF recognizes the need to address the educational challenges faced by the children of the widows who end up dropping out because of lack of school fees. Without support, many children are unable to continue their education, leading to social issues within the community.

GRF looks forward to creating pathways for these children's education. We aim to act as a bridge, connecting them with resources and opportunities.

Key Objectives:

  • Prevent school dropouts due to financial constraints
  • Connect children with educational sponsors
  • Create pathways to continued education
  • Break the cycle of poverty through education

2. Water Catchment Areas

One of the critical challenges in this region is its semi-arid climate, characterized by irregular weather patterns. Rainfall occurs only two to three times a year, severely impacting crop production and limiting access to water.

Many women, including the elderly, must travel long distances to fetch water, often carrying it on their backs. Additionally, they need to take their livestock far from home to access water.

Looking for partners to help fund:

  • Drilling of boreholes in the community
  • Water tanks for each woman to store rainwater
  • Water catchment systems

Due to a lack of resources, many widows cannot afford clean drinking water. Purchasing a 20-liter bottle costs Ksh. 20 or more, which is often beyond their means. As a result, they rely on water from nearby rivers, which is not safe and poses health risks.

3. Climate Justice

"It's the little things citizens do. That's what will make the difference. My little thing is planting trees." - Wangari Maathai, Nobel Peace Prize Laureate

Deforestation has significantly decreased tropical rainfall due to the widespread use of trees for firewood and charcoal. GINA Royal Foundation aims to be environmental ambassadors by promoting a greener future.

We intend to educate these women on the importance of planting trees and other vegetation, such as bamboo, to help restore the ecosystem and also introduce regenerative farming inclusive of using renewable energy sources to conserve the environment.

Key Initiatives:

  • Tree planting programs
  • Bamboo cultivation
  • Regenerative farming techniques
  • Renewable energy adoption

4. The Art of Weaving

GRF seeks to create additional income streams through the art of "viondo" basketry. This skill of utilizing local sisal wool will be taught to the widows, providing them with a valuable craft and a means to financially support their families.

By planting the sisal vegetation they will promote climate justice and create their own ready resource materials. This creates a sustainable cycle: grow the materials, craft the baskets, sell for income, and contribute to environmental restoration.

Program Components:

  • Experienced trainers in traditional basket weaving
  • Partnerships for skill development
  • Market establishment for basket sales
  • Sisal vegetation planting for sustainability

5. Healing, Counseling and Medical Camps

GRF recognizes the emotional and psychological impact of loss. We aim to bring in professional counselors alongside widows who have experienced healing and are using their journeys to inspire and support others.

By sharing their stories, these women will demonstrate that it is possible to rise from adversity and find hope, providing encouragement and guidance to those still navigating their pain.

Some widows range in age from 30 to 90 years, and many face health challenges without the financial means to seek medical care. To address this critical need GRF looks forward to organizing medical camps which will provide services such as eye care and diabetes screenings, among others, ensuring these women receive the healthcare they need.

Services Provided:

  • Professional grief counseling
  • Peer support from healed widows
  • Medical camps with free healthcare
  • Eye care services
  • Diabetes screenings
  • General health checkups

By combining professional counseling with the lived experiences of healed widows and proactively addressing healthcare needs through medical camps, GRF aims to provide holistic support, fostering both emotional well-being and physical health.
